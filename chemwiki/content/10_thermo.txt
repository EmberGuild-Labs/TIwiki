@CATEGORY Thermochemistry

@ARTICLE Energy and Heat
# Definitions
  system       what you are studying
  surroundings everything else
  heat q       energy transferred by a
               temperature difference
  work w       energy transferred by a
               force

# Units
  1 cal = 4.184 J
  1 Cal (food) = 1 kcal = 4184 J

# Exothermic
Releases heat to the surroundings.
  q is negative, dH < 0
The container feels HOT. Products are
lower in energy than reactants.
Combustion, neutralization, freezing.

# Endothermic
Absorbs heat from the surroundings.
  q is positive, dH > 0
The container feels COLD. Products are
higher in energy.
Melting, boiling, most instant cold
packs, photosynthesis.

# First law
Energy is conserved.
  dE = q + w

# Common confusion
Heat and temperature are different. Heat
is energy in transit; temperature
measures average kinetic energy. A
bathtub of warm water holds more heat
than a spark that is far hotter.

@ARTICLE Calorimetry
  q = m*c*dT
  dT = T_final - T_initial

# Specific heat
Energy to raise 1 g by 1 degree.
  water   4.184 J/(g*C)
  ethanol 2.44
  aluminum 0.897
  iron    0.449
  gold    0.129
Water's unusually high value moderates
climate and makes it a good coolant.

# Heat capacity
  C = m*c
For the whole object, in J/C.

# Coffee cup calorimeter
Constant pressure, so it measures dH.
  q_released = -q_absorbed
  q_reaction = -q_water

# Bomb calorimeter
Constant volume, so it measures dE.
  q = C_cal * dT

# Phase changes
  q = m*dH_fus  (melting/freezing)
  q = m*dH_vap  (boiling/condensing)
The temperature does NOT change during a
phase change.
  water dH_fus = 334 J/g
  water dH_vap = 2260 J/g

# Heating curve
Sloped segments use q = mcdT; flat
segments use q = m*dH. Solve a multi-step
problem one segment at a time.

@ARTICLE Enthalpy and Hess's Law
# Enthalpy change
  dH = H_products - H_reactants
Heat at constant pressure.
  dH < 0 exothermic
  dH > 0 endothermic

# Thermochemical equations
  CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O
  dH = -890 kJ
The value is per mole of the equation as
written, so it scales with the
coefficients.

# Manipulation rules
- Reverse the equation: flip the sign
of dH.
- Multiply the equation by n: multiply
dH by n.
- Add equations: add the dH values.

# Hess's law
dH is a state function, so it depends
only on the start and end points, not
the route. Any set of steps that sums to
the target reaction gives the right dH.

# Method
Line up the given equations, flipping
and scaling until the intermediates
cancel and the target remains, then add
the enthalpies.

# Formation enthalpies
  dH_rxn = sum(n*dHf products)
           - sum(n*dHf reactants)
dHf of any ELEMENT in its standard state
is exactly 0.

# Bond energies
  dH = sum(bonds broken)
       - sum(bonds formed)
Breaking bonds costs energy; forming
them releases it.

@ARTICLE Entropy and Free Energy
# Entropy S
A measure of disorder, or the number of
ways to arrange the energy. Units
J/(mol*K).

# Entropy increases when
- solid -> liquid -> gas
- moles of gas increase
- a solid dissolves
- temperature rises
Gases have far more entropy than solids.

# Predicting the sign of dS
Count moles of gas on each side. More
gas on the right means dS is positive.

# Gibbs free energy
  dG = dH - T*dS
T must be in kelvin, and dS is usually
given in J while dH is in kJ, so watch
the units.

# Spontaneity
  dG < 0  spontaneous
  dG = 0  at equilibrium
  dG > 0  nonspontaneous (the reverse
          is spontaneous)

# The four cases
  dH < 0, dS > 0: always spontaneous
  dH > 0, dS < 0: never spontaneous
  dH < 0, dS < 0: spontaneous at LOW T
  dH > 0, dS > 0: spontaneous at HIGH T

# Crossover temperature
  T = dH/dS
The temperature where dG changes sign.

# Spontaneous is not fast
Diamond turning to graphite is
spontaneous and takes geological time.
Spontaneity is thermodynamics; speed is
kinetics.

# Second and third laws
The entropy of the universe always
increases. A perfect crystal at 0 K has
zero entropy.
