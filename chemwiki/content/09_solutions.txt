@CATEGORY Solutions

@ARTICLE Solutions and Solubility
# Terms
  solute    the substance dissolved
  solvent   what does the dissolving
  solution  the homogeneous mixture

# Like dissolves like
Polar and ionic solutes dissolve in
polar solvents such as water. Nonpolar
solutes dissolve in nonpolar solvents
such as hexane. Oil and water separate
for this reason.

# How dissolving works
Water molecules surround an ion and pull
it away from the lattice. The ion ends
up hydrated, with the partial negative
oxygens facing cations.

# Saturation
  unsaturated  more can dissolve
  saturated    at the limit, with
               solid in equilibrium
  supersaturated  more than the limit,
               unstable, crystallizes
               when disturbed

# Electrolytes
  strong: fully ionizes, conducts well
  (soluble salts, strong acids/bases)
  weak: partly ionizes, conducts poorly
  (weak acids and bases)
  nonelectrolyte: does not ionize
  (sugar, ethanol)

# Factors affecting solubility
- Solids: usually more soluble in hot
solvent
- Gases: LESS soluble when hot, and more
soluble at high pressure (Henry's law).
Warm soda goes flat.

@ARTICLE Concentration
# Molarity (the main one)
  M = moles solute/liters solution
Note: liters of SOLUTION, not of
solvent.

# Molality
  m = moles solute/kg solvent
Uses kilograms of SOLVENT. Preferred for
colligative properties because it does
not change with temperature.

# Percent by mass
  % = (mass solute/mass solution)*100

# Percent by volume
  % = (vol solute/vol solution)*100

# Parts per million
  ppm = (mass solute/mass solution)*1e6
For dilute water solutions, 1 ppm is
about 1 mg per liter.

# Mole fraction
  X_A = n_A/n_total

# Getting moles from molarity
  moles = M * V(L)
This is the bridge into stoichiometry
for anything in solution.

@ARTICLE Dilution
  M1*V1 = M2*V2
Adding solvent changes the volume and
the concentration but NOT the moles of
solute.

# Using it
1 and 2 are the concentrated and dilute
states. Any volume unit works as long as
both sides match.

# Example
How much 12.0 M HCl makes 500. mL of
0.100 M?
  V1 = (0.100)(500.)/12.0 = 4.17 mL
Then add water up to 500 mL.

# Safety
Always add ACID to WATER, never the
reverse. The mixing is strongly
exothermic and can spatter.

# Serial dilution
Repeated dilutions multiply:
  a 1:10 done three times is 1:1000

# Mixing two solutions
  M_final = (M1*V1 + M2*V2)/(V1 + V2)

@ARTICLE Solution Stoichiometry
Solutions enter the mole map through
molarity.

  volume * molarity -> MOLES -> ratio
  -> moles of the other substance

# Titration
A measured reaction used to find an
unknown concentration.

# Endpoint vs equivalence point
The equivalence point is where the moles
match stoichiometrically. The endpoint is
where the indicator changes. A good
indicator makes them nearly identical.

# Acid-base titration
  M_a*V_a*n_a = M_b*V_b*n_b
n is the number of H+ or OH- per
formula unit. For a monoprotic acid with
a single hydroxide base this reduces to
  M_a*V_a = M_b*V_b

# Example
25.0 mL of HCl needs 32.0 mL of 0.100 M
NaOH:
  M_a = (0.100)(32.0)/25.0 = 0.128 M

# Standard solution
A solution of accurately known
concentration, used as the reference.

@ARTICLE Colligative Properties
Properties that depend on the NUMBER of
dissolved particles, not their identity.

# Boiling point elevation
  dTb = i*Kb*m
  water Kb = 0.512 C/m

# Freezing point depression
  dTf = i*Kf*m
  water Kf = 1.86 C/m
This is why salt melts ice and antifreeze
protects an engine.

# Van't Hoff factor i
The number of particles one formula unit
produces.
  sugar (nonelectrolyte)  i = 1
  NaCl                    i = 2
  CaCl2                   i = 3
  Al2(SO4)3               i = 5
Real values run slightly below these
because of ion pairing.

# Vapor pressure lowering
  P_solution = X_solvent * P_pure
Raoult's law. A nonvolatile solute
lowers the vapor pressure, which is what
raises the boiling point.

# Osmotic pressure
  pi = i*M*R*T
Water flows through a semipermeable
membrane toward the more concentrated
side.

# Comparing solutions
For the same molality, the solute with
the largest i has the biggest effect.
