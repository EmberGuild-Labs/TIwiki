@CATEGORY Naming and Formulas

@ARTICLE Polyatomic Ions
Memorize these; almost every naming and
reaction problem uses them.

# Charge 1-
  acetate          C2H3O2-
  bicarbonate      HCO3-
  bisulfate        HSO4-
  chlorate         ClO3-
  chlorite         ClO2-
  cyanide          CN-
  dihydrogen phos. H2PO4-
  hydroxide        OH-
  hypochlorite     ClO-
  nitrate          NO3-
  nitrite          NO2-
  perchlorate      ClO4-
  permanganate     MnO4-

# Charge 2-
  carbonate        CO3 2-
  chromate         CrO4 2-
  dichromate       Cr2O7 2-
  hydrogen phos.   HPO4 2-
  oxalate          C2O4 2-
  peroxide         O2 2-
  sulfate          SO4 2-
  sulfite          SO3 2-
  thiosulfate      S2O3 2-

# Charge 3-
  phosphate        PO4 3-
  phosphite        PO3 3-

# Charge 1+
  ammonium         NH4+
  hydronium        H3O+

# The -ate / -ite pattern
The -ite ion has ONE fewer oxygen than
the -ate ion, with the same charge.
  sulfate SO4 2-, sulfite SO3 2-

# The per- / hypo- pattern
  perchlorate   ClO4-   (one more O)
  chlorate      ClO3-
  chlorite      ClO2-
  hypochlorite  ClO-    (one fewer O)

# Adding hydrogen
Each H added raises the charge by 1.
  PO4 3- -> HPO4 2- -> H2PO4-

@ARTICLE Naming Ionic Compounds
Metal first, then nonmetal. The name
never uses prefixes.

# Simple binary
Name the metal, then the nonmetal root
plus -ide.
  NaCl    sodium chloride
  MgO     magnesium oxide
  Al2S3   aluminum sulfide

# With a polyatomic ion
Keep the polyatomic name as it is.
  Na2SO4  sodium sulfate
  CaCO3   calcium carbonate
  NH4Cl   ammonium chloride

# Transition metals: Roman numerals
The numeral gives the charge on the
metal, NOT the number of atoms.
  FeCl2   iron(II) chloride
  FeCl3   iron(III) chloride
  Cu2O    copper(I) oxide

# Finding the charge
Work backwards from the anion.
  Fe2O3: 3 oxygens at 2- is 6-, so two
  irons share 6+, giving Fe3+
  = iron(III) oxide

# No numeral needed
Group 1, group 2, Al, Zn, Ag and Cd have
only one common charge.

# Writing formulas from names
Balance total positive against total
negative, then reduce the subscripts.
Use parentheses around a polyatomic ion
that needs a subscript:
  calcium nitrate = Ca(NO3)2

@ARTICLE Naming Covalent Compounds
Two nonmetals. Here you DO use prefixes.

# Prefixes
  1 mono    6 hexa
  2 di      7 hepta
  3 tri     8 octa
  4 tetra   9 nona
  5 penta  10 deca

# Rules
- First element keeps its name; skip
"mono" on the first element.
- Second element gets the -ide ending
and always takes a prefix.
- Drop a trailing vowel before oxide:
monooxide becomes monoxide.

# Examples
  CO     carbon monoxide
  CO2    carbon dioxide
  N2O4   dinitrogen tetroxide
  PCl5   phosphorus pentachloride
  SF6    sulfur hexafluoride
  P4O10  tetraphosphorus decoxide

# Common names that stay
  H2O   water
  NH3   ammonia
  CH4   methane
  H2O2  hydrogen peroxide

# The big distinction
Metal + nonmetal: ionic, no prefixes,
maybe a Roman numeral.
Nonmetal + nonmetal: covalent, prefixes,
never a Roman numeral.

@ARTICLE Naming Acids
An acid starts with H in the formula.

# No oxygen: hydro- ...-ic acid
  HCl    hydrochloric acid
  HBr    hydrobromic acid
  HI     hydroiodic acid
  HF     hydrofluoric acid
  H2S    hydrosulfuric acid
  HCN    hydrocyanic acid

# With oxygen, from an -ate ion: -ic
  HNO3    nitric acid      (nitrate)
  H2SO4   sulfuric acid    (sulfate)
  H3PO4   phosphoric acid  (phosphate)
  H2CO3   carbonic acid    (carbonate)
  HClO3   chloric acid     (chlorate)
  HC2H3O2 acetic acid      (acetate)

# With oxygen, from an -ite ion: -ous
  HNO2   nitrous acid      (nitrite)
  H2SO3  sulfurous acid    (sulfite)
  HClO2  chlorous acid     (chlorite)

# The memory line
  -ate becomes -ic
  -ite becomes -ous
  no oxygen becomes hydro- ...-ic

# Bases
Metal hydroxides, named as ordinary
ionic compounds.
  NaOH   sodium hydroxide
  Ca(OH)2 calcium hydroxide

@ARTICLE Hydrates and Formula Writing
# Hydrates
Ionic compounds with water trapped in
the crystal. Use a prefix plus
"hydrate".
  CuSO4 * 5H2O
  copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate
  CoCl2 * 6H2O
  cobalt(II) chloride hexahydrate

# Anhydrous
The same compound with the water driven
off by heating. The color often changes:
blue CuSO4*5H2O turns white.

# Percent water in a hydrate
  % = (mass of water/total mass)*100

# Crossover rule
Write each ion with its charge, then
swap the charge numbers to become the
subscripts, then reduce.
  Al3+ and O2-  ->  Al2O3
  Mg2+ and O2-  ->  Mg2O2  ->  MgO

# Parentheses
Needed only when a polyatomic ion takes
a subscript greater than one.
  Fe(NO3)3, (NH4)2SO4, Ca(OH)2

# Checking a formula
The total positive charge must exactly
cancel the total negative charge, and
the subscripts must be in lowest terms
for an ionic compound.
