@CATEGORY Electrochemistry and Nuclear

@ARTICLE Balancing Redox
# Half reactions
Split into oxidation and reduction, then
balance each separately.

# Steps in acidic solution
1. Balance all atoms except O and H.
2. Balance O by adding H2O.
3. Balance H by adding H+.
4. Balance charge by adding electrons.
5. Scale the halves so the electrons
cancel.
6. Add them together and simplify.

# In basic solution
Do all of the above, then add enough OH-
to BOTH sides to neutralize every H+,
turning them into water. Cancel any
water that appears on both sides.

# Check
Both the atoms and the total charge must
balance in the final equation.

# Terms
The oxidized species loses electrons and
is the reducing agent. The reduced
species gains electrons and is the
oxidizing agent.

@ARTICLE Galvanic Cells
A spontaneous redox reaction that pushes
electrons through a wire.

# The electrodes
  ANODE:   OXIDATION, negative in a
           galvanic cell
  CATHODE: REDUCTION, positive
Remember "AN OX" and "RED CAT", and that
the vowels match: Anode/Oxidation,
Cathode/Reduction.

# Electron flow
Always from anode to cathode through the
external wire.

# Salt bridge
Carries ions to keep both halves
electrically neutral. Anions travel
toward the anode, cations toward the
cathode. Without it the cell stops
almost at once.

# Cell notation
  anode | anode ion || cathode ion
  | cathode
  Zn | Zn2+ || Cu2+ | Cu
The double line is the salt bridge.

# Cell potential
  E_cell = E_cathode - E_anode
using standard reduction potentials for
both.
  E > 0  spontaneous (galvanic)
  E < 0  needs an external supply

# Some standard potentials (V)
  F2 + 2e- -> 2F-        +2.87
  Cl2 + 2e- -> 2Cl-      +1.36
  Ag+ + e- -> Ag         +0.80
  Cu2+ + 2e- -> Cu       +0.34
  2H+ + 2e- -> H2         0.00
  Zn2+ + 2e- -> Zn       -0.76
  Li+ + e- -> Li         -3.04

# Important
Do NOT multiply E by the number of
electrons when scaling a half reaction.
Potential is intensive.

@ARTICLE Electrolysis and Free Energy
# Electrolytic cells
Use electricity to force a
nonspontaneous reaction. The signs of
the electrodes flip: the anode is
positive and the cathode is negative,
but oxidation still happens at the anode.

# Uses
Electroplating, refining aluminum from
bauxite, splitting water into H2 and O2,
recharging a battery.

# Faraday's laws
  1 F = 96485 C/mol of electrons
  Q = I*t   (coulombs)
  moles of e- = I*t/96485
Then use the half reaction ratio to get
moles of product.

# Linking to thermodynamics
  dG = -n*F*E
n is moles of electrons transferred.
A positive E means a negative dG, so
spontaneous.

# Nernst equation
  E = E0 - (0.0592/n)*log(Q)  at 25 C
Cell potential falls as the reaction
proceeds and Q rises. At equilibrium
E = 0 and the battery is dead.

# Relating E0 and K
  log K = n*E0/0.0592

# Corrosion
Rusting is iron oxidized by oxygen.
Galvanizing coats steel with zinc, which
is more active and corrodes first as a
sacrificial anode.

@ARTICLE Nuclear Chemistry
# Types of decay
  alpha: emits He-4
    A drops by 4, Z drops by 2
  beta minus: neutron -> proton + e-
    A unchanged, Z rises by 1
  beta plus (positron)
    A unchanged, Z drops by 1
  gamma: photon only
    A and Z unchanged
  electron capture: p + e- -> n
    A unchanged, Z drops by 1

# Penetrating power
  alpha: stopped by paper or skin
  beta: stopped by thin aluminum
  gamma: needs thick lead or concrete
Alpha is the least penetrating but the
most damaging if inhaled or swallowed.

# Balancing nuclear equations
The mass numbers must sum equally on
both sides, and so must the atomic
numbers. Use that to identify the
missing particle.

# Half-life
  N = N0*(1/2)^(t/t_half)
After n half-lives, (1/2)^n remains.
Half-life is unaffected by temperature,
pressure or chemical form.

# Band of stability
Light nuclei are stable near a 1:1
neutron to proton ratio; heavy ones need
about 1.5:1. Everything above bismuth-83
is radioactive.

# Fission and fusion
Fission splits a heavy nucleus, releasing
neutrons that can sustain a chain
reaction. Fusion joins light nuclei and
powers stars. Both convert mass to energy
by E = mc^2.

# Uses
Carbon-14 dating (5730 y), medical
tracers, radiation therapy, smoke
detectors (americium-241).
