@CATEGORY The Periodic Table

@ARTICLE Organization of the Table
# Layout
  period = row = shell number
  group  = vertical column = same
           valence electrons
Elements in a group behave alike because
they have the same valence arrangement.

# Blocks
  groups 1-2   s block
  groups 13-18 p block
  groups 3-12  d block (transition)
  bottom rows  f block (lanthanides and
               actinides)

# Metals, nonmetals, metalloids
Metals are on the left and center;
nonmetals on the upper right; metalloids
form the staircase between them (B, Si,
Ge, As, Sb, Te).

# Metal properties
Shiny, conduct heat and electricity,
malleable, ductile, LOSE electrons to
form cations.

# Nonmetal properties
Dull, brittle, poor conductors, GAIN or
share electrons to form anions.

# Mendeleev
Arranged by mass and left gaps for
undiscovered elements, predicting their
properties. Moseley later reordered by
atomic number, fixing the anomalies.

@ARTICLE Groups and Families
# Group 1: alkali metals
Li Na K Rb Cs Fr
One valence electron, form 1+ ions, very
reactive, soft, react vigorously with
water. Reactivity INCREASES down the
group. Never found free in nature.

# Group 2: alkaline earth metals
Be Mg Ca Sr Ba Ra
Two valence electrons, form 2+ ions,
reactive but less so than group 1.

# Groups 3-12: transition metals
Form colored compounds, often have
multiple oxidation states (hence Roman
numerals in names), many are good
catalysts, generally hard with high
melting points.

# Group 17: halogens
F Cl Br I At
Seven valence electrons, form 1- ions,
very reactive nonmetals, diatomic.
Reactivity DECREASES down the group.
Fluorine is the most reactive nonmetal.

# Group 18: noble gases
He Ne Ar Kr Xe Rn
Full valence shell, essentially inert,
monatomic gases.

# Diatomic elements
  H2 N2 O2 F2 Cl2 Br2 I2
Remember "HONClBrIF" or the "7 up"
pattern on the table.

@ARTICLE Periodic Trends
# Atomic radius
  decreases across a period ->
  increases down a group
Across a period the nuclear charge grows
while the shell stays the same, so
electrons are pulled in tighter. Down a
group a whole new shell is added.

# Ionization energy
Energy to remove an electron.
  increases across a period ->
  decreases down a group
Opposite of radius: smaller atoms hold
their electrons more tightly. Noble
gases have the highest.

# Electronegativity
Pull on shared electrons in a bond.
  increases across a period ->
  decreases down a group
Fluorine is the highest at 4.0. Noble
gases are usually not assigned values.

# Electron affinity
Energy change when an electron is added.
Roughly follows electronegativity;
halogens are the most favorable.

# Metallic character
  decreases across a period ->
  increases down a group
The exact opposite of electronegativity.

# The one-line summary
Everything except radius and metallic
character increases up and to the right,
toward fluorine.

@ARTICLE Ionic Radius and Shielding
# Cations are smaller
Losing electrons often removes an entire
shell, and the remaining electrons are
pulled in harder.
  Na is 186 pm, Na+ is 102 pm

# Anions are larger
Adding electrons increases repulsion and
spreads the cloud out.
  Cl is 99 pm, Cl- is 181 pm

# Isoelectronic series
Species with the same electron count.
Radius shrinks as nuclear charge rises:
  N3- > O2- > F- > Ne > Na+ > Mg2+

# Effective nuclear charge
  Z_eff = Z - (shielding electrons)
Inner electrons shield the outer ones
from the nucleus. Across a period Z_eff
rises, which drives most trends.

# Successive ionization energies
Each removal costs more. A huge jump
appears once you break into a full
shell, which reveals the valence count.

@ARTICLE Common Ions by Group
# Predictable charges
  group 1   1+   Na+, K+
  group 2   2+   Mg2+, Ca2+
  group 13  3+   Al3+
  group 14  4+/4-  (usually covalent)
  group 15  3-   N3-, P3-
  group 16  2-   O2-, S2-
  group 17  1-   F-, Cl-, Br-, I-
  group 18  none

# Why
Atoms gain or lose electrons to reach a
noble gas configuration: the octet rule.

# Always these charges
  Ag+     Zn2+    Cd2+
  Al3+    Sc3+

# Multiple charges (Roman numerals)
  Fe2+ Fe3+     iron(II), iron(III)
  Cu+  Cu2+     copper(I), copper(II)
  Pb2+ Pb4+     lead(II), lead(IV)
  Sn2+ Sn4+     tin(II), tin(IV)
  Cr2+ Cr3+     Mn2+ Mn4+
  Co2+ Co3+     Hg2^2+ Hg2+
