@CATEGORY Gases

@ARTICLE Gas Laws
Temperature must be in KELVIN for every
one of these.

# Boyle's law (T, n fixed)
  P1*V1 = P2*V2
Pressure and volume are inversely
related. Squeeze it and the pressure
rises.

# Charles's law (P, n fixed)
  V1/T1 = V2/T2
Heat a balloon and it expands.

# Gay-Lussac's law (V, n fixed)
  P1/T1 = P2/T2
Heating a sealed can raises the
pressure, which is why aerosols warn
against fire.

# Avogadro's law (P, T fixed)
  V1/n1 = V2/n2
Equal volumes of gas hold equal numbers
of molecules.

# Combined gas law
  P1*V1/T1 = P2*V2/T2
Covers all of the above; drop whatever
stays constant.

# Pressure units
  1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr
  1 atm = 101.3 kPa = 101325 Pa
  1 atm = 14.7 psi

@ARTICLE Ideal Gas Law
  PV = nRT

# R depends on the pressure unit
  0.0821 L*atm/(mol*K)
  8.314 J/(mol*K)
  62.4 L*mmHg/(mol*K)
Pick the R whose units match your data.

# Rearrangements
  n = PV/(RT)
  molar mass = mRT/(PV)
  density = PM/(RT)
That last one shows gas density rises
with pressure and molar mass and falls
with temperature.

# STP
  0 C (273.15 K) and 1 atm
  1 mol of any gas = 22.4 L

# SATP
  25 C and 1 bar, molar volume 24.8 L
Check which your course uses.

# Assumptions of an ideal gas
- Particles have negligible volume
- No attractive forces between them
- Collisions are perfectly elastic
- Average kinetic energy is proportional
to the kelvin temperature

@ARTICLE Partial Pressures
# Dalton's law
  P_total = P1 + P2 + P3 + ...
Each gas in a mixture exerts pressure
independently of the others.

# Mole fraction
  X_A = n_A/n_total
  P_A = X_A * P_total
Mole fractions add to 1.

# Collecting a gas over water
The gas is mixed with water vapor:
  P_gas = P_total - P_water
The vapor pressure of water depends only
on the temperature and is looked up in a
table (23.8 mmHg at 25 C).

# Why it matters
Forgetting to subtract the water vapor
is the most common error in gas
collection labs.

@ARTICLE Kinetic Theory and Effusion
# Average kinetic energy
  KE_avg = (3/2)*k_B*T
Depends ONLY on temperature. At the same
temperature, every gas has the same
average kinetic energy.

# Root mean square speed
  u_rms = sqrt(3RT/M)
M is the molar mass in kg/mol. Lighter
molecules move faster at the same
temperature.

# Graham's law
  rate1/rate2 = sqrt(M2/M1)
Rate of effusion is inversely
proportional to the square root of the
molar mass. Helium escapes a balloon
much faster than air.

# Diffusion vs effusion
Diffusion is spreading through another
gas; effusion is escaping through a tiny
hole. Graham's law describes both.

# Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
A curve of molecular speeds. Raising the
temperature flattens and widens it and
shifts the peak to the right; the area
under it stays constant.

@ARTICLE Real Gases
Real gases deviate from ideal behavior
at HIGH pressure and LOW temperature.

# Why
- At high pressure the molecules' own
volume is no longer negligible.
- At low temperature the attractions
between molecules matter, so they hit
the walls less hard.

# Van der Waals equation
  (P + a*n^2/V^2)(V - n*b) = nRT
a corrects for attraction, b for
molecular volume. Both are larger for
bigger, more polar molecules.

# Which gases behave most ideally
Small, nonpolar, light gases: He, H2,
Ne. Gases like NH3 and H2O deviate the
most because they hydrogen bond.

# Rule of thumb
The ideal gas law is good to within a
few percent at ordinary temperatures and
pressures near 1 atm.

@ARTICLE Gas Stoichiometry
Gases join the usual mole map through
volume.

# At STP
  1 mol = 22.4 L
Use it exactly like molar mass, as a
conversion factor.

# Not at STP
Convert with the ideal gas law:
  n = PV/(RT)
then continue with mole ratios.

# Volume-volume shortcut
At the same temperature and pressure,
volumes of gases combine in the same
ratio as the coefficients.
  N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3
So 1 L of N2 needs 3 L of H2 and gives
2 L of NH3, with no mole conversion
needed.

# Example
Liters of O2 at STP to burn 5.00 g CH4?
  CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O
  5.00/16.04 = 0.312 mol CH4
  * 2 = 0.623 mol O2
  * 22.4 L/mol = 14.0 L
