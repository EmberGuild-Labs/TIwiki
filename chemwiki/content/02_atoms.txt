@CATEGORY Atomic Structure

@ARTICLE Subatomic Particles
  particle  charge  mass (u)  location
  proton      +1     1.007    nucleus
  neutron      0     1.009    nucleus
  electron    -1     0.00055  outside

# Key facts
- The electron is about 1836 times
lighter than the proton, so essentially
all the mass is in the nucleus.
- The nucleus is about 1/100,000 the
diameter of the atom, so the atom is
overwhelmingly empty space.
- A neutral atom has equal numbers of
protons and electrons.

# What each determines
  protons   -> which element it is
  neutrons  -> which isotope
  electrons -> chemical behavior and
               charge

# History
- Dalton: indivisible atoms
- Thomson: found the electron, plum
pudding model
- Rutherford: gold foil experiment,
found the tiny dense nucleus
- Bohr: electrons in quantized orbits
- Modern: probability clouds, orbitals

@ARTICLE Isotopes and Atomic Mass
# Notation
  A = mass number = protons + neutrons
  Z = atomic number = protons
  neutrons = A - Z

Written as carbon-14, or with A on top
and Z on the bottom left.

# Isotopes
Same element, different neutron count.
Same chemistry, different mass, and
sometimes radioactive.
  C-12, C-13, C-14 all have 6 protons

# Average atomic mass
  sum of (mass * fractional abundance)
This is the number on the periodic
table, which is why it is rarely a whole
number.

# Example
Chlorine is 75.77% Cl-35 and 24.23%
Cl-37:
  0.7577(34.97) + 0.2423(36.97)
  = 35.45 u

# Ions
Adding or removing electrons makes an
ion; it does NOT change the element or
the mass number.
  cation  = positive = lost electrons
  anion   = negative = gained electrons

@ARTICLE Electron Configuration
# Order of filling
  1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p
  6s 4f 5d 6p 7s 5f 6d 7p

# Capacity
  s holds 2    p holds 6
  d holds 10   f holds 14

# The three rules
- Aufbau: fill the lowest energy orbital
first.
- Pauli exclusion: at most 2 electrons
per orbital, and they must have opposite
spins.
- Hund's rule: fill every orbital in a
subshell singly before pairing any.

# Examples
  O  (8):  1s2 2s2 2p4
  Na (11): 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s1
  Fe (26): [Ar] 4s2 3d6

# Noble gas shorthand
Replace the inner electrons with the
previous noble gas in brackets.
  Cl: [Ne] 3s2 3p5

# Two famous exceptions
  Cr: [Ar] 4s1 3d5
  Cu: [Ar] 4s1 3d10
Half-filled and filled d subshells are
extra stable.

# Ions
Take electrons from the highest n first.
For transition metals that means the s
electrons go BEFORE the d electrons.
  Fe2+: [Ar] 3d6

@ARTICLE Quantum Numbers and Orbitals
# The four quantum numbers
  n  principal: shell, 1, 2, 3...
     sets size and energy
  l  angular: subshell, 0 to n-1
     0=s, 1=p, 2=d, 3=f
  m_l  magnetic: orientation, -l to +l
  m_s  spin: +1/2 or -1/2

No two electrons in one atom share all
four.

# Orbital shapes
  s: sphere, 1 orientation
  p: dumbbell, 3 orientations
  d: cloverleaf mostly, 5 orientations
  f: complex, 7 orientations

# Electrons per shell
  max = 2n^2
  n=1: 2   n=2: 8   n=3: 18   n=4: 32

# Valence electrons
The electrons in the outermost shell.
They determine bonding. For main group
elements, the group number gives the
count (group 1 has 1, group 17 has 7).

# Orbital diagrams
Boxes or lines with arrows. Hund's rule
means all arrows point up in a subshell
before any pair off.
