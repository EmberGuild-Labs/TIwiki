@CATEGORY Momentum

@ARTICLE Momentum and Impulse
# Momentum
  p = m*v
A vector, in kg*m/s, pointing along the
velocity.

# Impulse
  J = F*t = change in p
  J = m*v_f - m*v_i
Impulse is also a vector, in N*s.

# Impulse-momentum theorem
  F_avg * t = m*(v_f - v_i)
A larger contact time means a smaller
force for the same change in momentum.
That is the physics of airbags, crumple
zones, catching a ball by pulling your
hands back, and bending your knees on
landing.

# From a graph
  impulse = area under an F vs t graph

# Newton's second law, general form
  F = dp/dt
This form still works when the mass
changes, such as for a rocket.

@ARTICLE Conservation of Momentum
If the net external force on a system is
zero, the total momentum is constant.
  p_before = p_after
  m1*v1i + m2*v2i = m1*v1f + m2*v2f

# Why it holds
Internal forces come in third-law pairs
that cancel within the system.

# It is a vector law
Conserve x and y momentum separately.

# Momentum vs energy
Momentum is conserved in EVERY collision.
Kinetic energy is conserved only in
elastic ones.

# Explosions and recoil
Starting from rest, the total momentum
stays zero:
  0 = m1*v1 + m2*v2
  v1 = -m2*v2/m1
The lighter piece moves faster. This is
recoil of a gun, or a rocket.

@ARTICLE Collisions
# Elastic
Both momentum and kinetic energy are
conserved. Objects bounce apart.
  m1*v1i + m2*v2i = m1*v1f + m2*v2f
  v1i - v2i = -(v1f - v2f)
The second equation says the relative
speed of approach equals the relative
speed of separation.

# Perfectly inelastic
The objects stick together and move with
one final velocity. Kinetic energy is
lost, momentum is not.
  m1*v1i + m2*v2i = (m1 + m2)*v_f

# Inelastic
Momentum is conserved, some kinetic
energy is lost, but the objects do not
stick.

# Energy lost
  lost = KE_before - KE_after
It becomes heat, sound and deformation.

# Special elastic cases
- Equal masses: they swap velocities.
- Heavy hits light at rest: the light one
leaves at nearly 2*v.
- Light hits heavy at rest: the light one
bounces back at nearly the same speed.

@ARTICLE Ballistic Pendulum
A two-stage problem, and a classic trap.

# Stage 1: the collision
Momentum is conserved, energy is not:
  m*v = (m + M)*V

# Stage 2: the swing
Energy is conserved, momentum is not
(gravity is external):
  (1/2)(m + M)*V^2 = (m + M)*g*h

# Result
  v = ((m + M)/m) * sqrt(2gh)

# The lesson
Do not apply conservation of energy
across the collision, and do not apply
conservation of momentum across the
swing. Split the problem at the moment
the collision ends.

@ARTICLE Center of Mass
  x_cm = (m1*x1 + m2*x2 + ...)/(sum m)
Do the same separately for y.

# Motion
  v_cm = (sum of m*v)/(sum of m)
With no external force, the center of
mass moves at constant velocity no matter
what happens inside the system. An
exploding shell's center of mass keeps
following the original parabola.

# Uniform shapes
The center of mass is at the geometric
center of a uniform symmetric object.

# Stability
An object tips over when its center of
mass passes outside its base of support.

# Two-body systems
The center of mass lies on the line
between them, closer to the heavier one,
dividing the distance in inverse
proportion to the masses.
