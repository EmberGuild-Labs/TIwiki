@CATEGORY Electrostatics

@ARTICLE Electric Charge
# Basics
  elementary charge e = 1.602e-19 C
  electron: -e     proton: +e
Charge is quantised: every charge is a
whole number of e.

# Rules
Like charges repel, opposite charges
attract. Charge is conserved; it is moved
around, never created.

# Conductors vs insulators
In a conductor, charge moves freely and
spreads over the outside surface. In an
insulator it stays put.

# Charging methods
- Friction: electrons rub off one object
onto another.
- Conduction: touching shares charge, so
both end up with the same sign.
- Induction: a nearby charge pushes
electrons to one side; ground it, remove
the ground, then remove the charge. The
result is the OPPOSITE sign.

# Polarisation
A neutral object is attracted to a
charged one because its charges shift
slightly, putting the opposite sign
nearer. This is why a balloon sticks to a
wall.

@ARTICLE Coulomb's Law
  F = k*q1*q2/r^2
  k = 8.99e9 N*m^2/C^2
  k = 1/(4*pi*epsilon0)
  epsilon0 = 8.85e-12 C^2/(N*m^2)

# Using it
Put the magnitudes in, get the magnitude
out, then decide the direction from
attract-or-repel. Do not fight with the
signs inside the formula.

# Inverse square
Doubling the separation quarters the
force, exactly like gravity. Coulomb's
law is far stronger: the electric force
between two protons is about 10^36 times
their gravitational attraction.

# Several charges
Superposition: work out each pairwise
force separately and add them as vectors.

# Equilibrium point
Between two like charges there is a point
where the net force on a third charge is
zero. Set the two magnitudes equal and
solve for the distance.

@ARTICLE Electric Field
  E = F/q
  E = k*Q/r^2   (point charge)
Units N/C or V/m. A vector.

# Direction
Field lines point away from positive
charge and toward negative. The field is
defined by the force on a POSITIVE test
charge.

# Force on a charge in a field
  F = qE
Positive charges are pushed along the
field, negative charges against it.

# Field lines
- They start on + and end on -.
- They never cross.
- Closer lines mean a stronger field.

# Uniform field (parallel plates)
  E = V/d
Constant between the plates, so a charge
there has constant acceleration and moves
just like a projectile.

# Inside a conductor
The field is zero inside a conductor in
equilibrium, and just outside it is
perpendicular to the surface. This is why
a car is a safe place in a lightning
storm (a Faraday cage).

@ARTICLE Gauss's Law
  flux = E*A*cos(theta)
  total flux = Q_enclosed/epsilon0

# Meaning
The electric flux through any closed
surface depends only on the charge inside
it, not on where that charge sits or what
else is outside.

# Choosing a surface
Pick a surface matching the symmetry so
E is constant and perpendicular:
- sphere for a point charge
- cylinder for a long line of charge
- pillbox for a large flat sheet

# Standard results
  point charge:  E = k*Q/r^2
  line charge:   E = 2*k*lambda/r
  sheet: E = sigma/(2*epsilon0)
  between plates: E = sigma/epsilon0

# Notice
The field of an infinite sheet does not
fall off with distance at all, and a line
charge falls off as 1/r rather than
1/r^2.

# Shells
Inside a uniform spherical shell of
charge the field is exactly zero. Outside
it looks like a point charge at the
center.

@ARTICLE Electric Potential
# Potential energy
  U = k*q1*q2/r
A scalar. Positive for like charges,
negative for opposite ones.

# Potential (voltage)
  V = U/q
  V = k*Q/r    (point charge)
  1 V = 1 J/C
A scalar, so add the contributions of
several charges as plain numbers, with
signs, and no vectors.

# Potential difference
  dV = V_b - V_a
  W = q*dV
The work done moving a charge between two
points.

# Field and potential
  E = -dV/dx
  V = E*d   (uniform field)
The field points from high to low
potential, in the direction V falls
fastest.

# Equipotentials
Surfaces of constant V. They are always
perpendicular to the field lines, and no
work is done moving along one.

# Electron volt
  1 eV = 1.602e-19 J
The energy an electron gains crossing 1
volt.

@ARTICLE Capacitors
  C = Q/V
  1 F = 1 C/V
A farad is huge; real capacitors are
usually microfarads or picofarads.

# Parallel plates
  C = epsilon0*A/d
  C = kappa*epsilon0*A/d   (dielectric)
kappa is the dielectric constant, always
greater than 1, so a dielectric raises
the capacitance.

# Energy stored
  U = (1/2)C*V^2
  U = (1/2)Q*V
  U = Q^2/(2C)

# Combinations
Note these are the OPPOSITE of resistors:
  parallel: C_eq = C1 + C2 + ...
  series:   1/C_eq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ...

# Reasoning about the combinations
In parallel the plate area effectively
adds, so the capacitance grows. In series
the same charge sits on each, the voltages
add, so the total capacitance falls.

# Charged and disconnected
Q stays fixed. Pulling the plates apart
raises V and raises the stored energy;
that energy comes from the work you do.
