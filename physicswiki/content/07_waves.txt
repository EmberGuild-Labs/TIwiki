@CATEGORY Oscillations and Waves

@ARTICLE Simple Harmonic Motion
SHM happens when the restoring force is
proportional to the displacement:
  F = -kx

# Position, velocity, acceleration
  x = A*cos(omega*t)
  v = -A*omega*sin(omega*t)
  a = -A*omega^2*cos(omega*t)
  a = -omega^2 * x

# Angular frequency
  omega = 2*pi*f = 2*pi/T

# Amplitude and period
A is the maximum displacement. For an
ideal oscillator the period does NOT
depend on the amplitude.

# Where things are extreme
- At x = 0: speed is maximum,
acceleration is zero
- At x = +-A: speed is zero,
acceleration is maximum

# Maximum values
  v_max = A*omega
  a_max = A*omega^2

# Energy
  E = (1/2)k*A^2  (constant)
  KE + PE = E at every instant

@ARTICLE Mass-Spring and Pendulum
# Mass on a spring
  T = 2*pi*sqrt(m/k)
  omega = sqrt(k/m)
More mass means a slower oscillation; a
stiffer spring means a faster one. The
period does not depend on gravity, so it
is the same on the Moon.

# Simple pendulum (small angles)
  T = 2*pi*sqrt(L/g)
  omega = sqrt(g/L)
The period does not depend on the mass,
and only on the length and g. The small
angle approximation sin(theta) ~ theta
needs roughly theta < 15 degrees.

# Physical pendulum
  T = 2*pi*sqrt(I/(m*g*d))
d is the distance from the pivot to the
center of mass.

# Damping
Friction gradually reduces the amplitude.
The period is nearly unchanged for light
damping.

# Resonance
Driving an oscillator at its natural
frequency produces a large amplitude.
This is how a swing is pumped, and why
soldiers break step on bridges.

@ARTICLE Wave Basics
# Wave equation
  v = f * lambda
  f = 1/T

# Terms
  amplitude   maximum displacement
  wavelength  distance between crests
  frequency   cycles per second, Hz
  period      seconds per cycle

# Transverse vs longitudinal
Transverse: the medium moves
perpendicular to the wave (light, waves
on a string).
Longitudinal: the medium moves along the
wave (sound), making compressions and
rarefactions.

# What sets the speed
The speed is a property of the MEDIUM,
not of the source.
  string: v = sqrt(F_T/mu)
where mu is mass per unit length.

# Crossing into a new medium
The frequency stays the same; the speed
and wavelength change together.

# Wave function
  y = A*sin(k*x - omega*t)
  k = 2*pi/lambda
  v = omega/k

@ARTICLE Superposition and Interference
# Principle of superposition
When waves overlap, the displacements
add. Afterwards each wave carries on
unchanged.

# Constructive interference
Crests line up with crests. The path
difference is a whole number of
wavelengths:
  path difference = n*lambda

# Destructive interference
A crest meets a trough. The path
difference is a half-odd number of
wavelengths:
  path difference = (n + 1/2)*lambda

# Phase
In phase means the crests coincide; out
of phase by 180 degrees means they
cancel.

# Beats
Two slightly different frequencies
produce a slow throbbing:
  f_beat = |f1 - f2|
Piano tuners listen for the beats to
vanish.

@ARTICLE Standing Waves
Formed when a wave interferes with its
own reflection. Nodes stay still,
antinodes swing the most.

# String fixed at both ends
  lambda_n = 2L/n,  n = 1, 2, 3...
  f_n = n*v/(2L) = n*f1
All harmonics are present.

# Pipe open at both ends
  lambda_n = 2L/n
  f_n = n*v/(2L)
Same pattern as the string; antinodes at
both open ends.

# Pipe closed at one end
  lambda_n = 4L/n,  n = 1, 3, 5...
  f_n = n*v/(4L)
Only ODD harmonics. The fundamental is
an octave lower than an open pipe of the
same length.

# Node spacing
Adjacent nodes are half a wavelength
apart.

# Fundamental and harmonics
  f1 is the fundamental
  f2 = 2*f1 is the second harmonic

@ARTICLE Sound
# Speed of sound in air
  v = 343 m/s at 20 degrees C
  v = 331 + 0.6*T   (T in Celsius)
Faster in liquids, faster still in
solids.

# What we hear
  pitch     <- frequency
  loudness  <- amplitude / intensity
  quality   <- harmonic content
Human hearing spans about 20 Hz to
20,000 Hz.

# Intensity
  I = P/(4*pi*r^2)
Follows an inverse square law from a
point source.

# Decibels
  dB = 10*log(I/I0)
  I0 = 1e-12 W/m^2
Every 10 dB is a factor of 10 in
intensity, heard as roughly twice as
loud.

# Echo and distance
  d = v*t/2
The sound travels there and back.

@ARTICLE Doppler Effect
The observed frequency shifts when the
source or observer moves.

  f' = f * (v +- v_o)/(v -+ v_s)

# Choosing signs
Use the signs that push the frequency in
the direction physical sense demands:
- Approaching: higher frequency, so use
+v_o on top or -v_s on the bottom.
- Receding: lower frequency.

# Everyday version
A siren rises in pitch as it comes toward
you and drops as it passes. The pitch
does not fall gradually the whole time;
it drops at the moment of passing.

# Light and redshift
Light shows the same effect. Galaxies
moving away are redshifted, which is the
core evidence for the expansion of the
universe.

# Sonic boom
When v_s equals v the waves pile up into
a shock front; beyond it the source is
supersonic and drags a cone of sound
behind it.
