@CATEGORY Fluids and Thermodynamics

@ARTICLE Density and Pressure
# Density
  rho = m/V
  water: 1000 kg/m^3 = 1 g/cm^3

# Pressure
  P = F/A
  1 Pa = 1 N/m^2
  1 atm = 101325 Pa = 101.3 kPa

# Pressure in a fluid
  P = P0 + rho*g*h
Depends only on the depth, not on the
shape or the amount of the container.
Every point at the same depth in a
connected fluid is at the same pressure.

# Gauge vs absolute
  P_gauge = P_absolute - P_atmosphere
A tyre gauge reads gauge pressure.

# Pascal's principle
Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is
transmitted undiminished throughout.
  F1/A1 = F2/A2
This is the hydraulic lift: a small force
on a small piston lifts a large load, at
the cost of a much longer stroke.

@ARTICLE Buoyancy
# Archimedes' principle
The buoyant force equals the weight of
the fluid displaced.
  F_b = rho_fluid * V_displaced * g

# Float, sink or hover
  rho_object < rho_fluid : floats
  rho_object > rho_fluid : sinks
  equal: hovers at any depth

# Floating object
The buoyant force equals the weight:
  rho_obj*V_obj = rho_fluid*V_sub
  V_sub/V_obj = rho_obj/rho_fluid
Ice is about 0.92 as dense as water, so
roughly 92% of an iceberg is underwater.

# Apparent weight
  W_apparent = W_actual - F_b
This is how the density of an irregular
object is measured.

# Common trap
The buoyant force does not depend on the
depth (for an incompressible fluid) or on
the mass of the object, only on the
volume displaced and the fluid density.

@ARTICLE Fluid Flow
# Continuity equation
  A1*v1 = A2*v2
Volume flow rate is constant, so a fluid
speeds up through a narrow section. This
is why a thumb over a hose makes a fast
jet.

# Flow rate
  Q = A*v   (m^3/s)

# Bernoulli's equation
  P + (1/2)rho*v^2 + rho*g*h = constant
Energy conservation per unit volume for
an ideal fluid.

# The key consequence
Where the speed is high, the pressure is
low. This explains lift on a wing, a
curveball, a shower curtain pulled
inward, and two passing ships drawn
together.

# Torricelli's theorem
Speed of fluid leaving a hole at depth h:
  v = sqrt(2gh)
The same as an object dropped that far.

# Assumptions
Ideal fluid: incompressible, no
viscosity, steady and non-turbulent flow.
Real fluids lose pressure to friction.

@ARTICLE Temperature and Expansion
# Scales
  C = (5/9)(F - 32)
  F = (9/5)C + 32
  K = C + 273.15
Absolute zero is 0 K = -273.15 C. Use
kelvin in every gas law.

# Thermal expansion
  linear:  dL = alpha*L0*dT
  area:    dA = 2*alpha*A0*dT
  volume:  dV = beta*V0*dT
  with beta ~ 3*alpha

# Consequences
Bridges need expansion joints, rails need
gaps, and a bimetallic strip bends
because the two metals expand by
different amounts.

# A hole expands too
A hole in a heated plate gets bigger, not
smaller. The material expands as if the
hole were made of the same stuff.

# Water is strange
Water expands when it freezes and is
densest at 4 degrees C, so ice floats and
lakes freeze from the top down.

@ARTICLE Heat and Calorimetry
# Heat vs temperature
Heat is energy in transit because of a
temperature difference. Temperature
measures average molecular kinetic
energy.

# Specific heat
  Q = m*c*dT
  water: c = 4186 J/(kg*C)
Water's large specific heat moderates
coastal climates.

# Phase changes
  Q = m*L
  L_f water = 3.34e5 J/kg  (fusion)
  L_v water = 2.26e6 J/kg  (boiling)
The temperature does NOT change during a
phase change; the energy goes into
breaking bonds.

# Calorimetry
In an insulated container,
  Q_lost = Q_gained
  sum of m*c*dT = 0

# Heat transfer
- Conduction: contact, through a solid
- Convection: bulk motion of a fluid
- Radiation: electromagnetic waves, no
medium required

# Conduction rate
  Q/t = k*A*dT/L

@ARTICLE Ideal Gas Law
  PV = nRT
  R = 8.314 J/(mol*K)

# Molecule form
  PV = N*k_B*T
  k_B = 1.381e-23 J/K

# Combined gas law
  P1*V1/T1 = P2*V2/T2
Temperature must be in kelvin.

# Special cases
  Boyle:   P1*V1 = P2*V2      (T fixed)
  Charles: V1/T1 = V2/T2      (P fixed)
  Gay-Lussac: P1/T1 = P2/T2   (V fixed)

# Kinetic theory
  KE_avg = (3/2)k_B*T
  v_rms = sqrt(3*k_B*T/m)
  v_rms = sqrt(3*R*T/M)
Temperature IS average translational
kinetic energy. Lighter molecules move
faster at the same temperature.

# Moles
  n = m/M
  1 mol = 6.022e23 particles

@ARTICLE Laws of Thermodynamics
# Zeroth law
If A and B are each in thermal
equilibrium with C, they are in
equilibrium with each other. This is what
makes a thermometer meaningful.

# First law
  dU = Q - W
Energy conservation for a gas. Q is heat
added TO the gas, W is work done BY the
gas.
  W = P*dV   (constant pressure)
  W = area under a PV curve

# Sign conventions
  Q > 0 heat added
  W > 0 gas expands and does work
  dU > 0 temperature rises

# Named processes
  isothermal:  dT = 0, so dU = 0, Q = W
  adiabatic:   Q = 0, so dU = -W
  isobaric:    P constant, W = P*dV
  isochoric:   dV = 0, so W = 0, dU = Q

# Internal energy of an ideal gas
  U = (3/2)n*R*T   (monatomic)
It depends on the temperature alone.

# Second law
Heat flows spontaneously from hot to
cold, never the reverse without work. No
engine can be perfectly efficient.

# Third law
Absolute zero cannot be reached in a
finite number of steps.

@ARTICLE Heat Engines and Entropy
# Efficiency
  e = W/Q_H = 1 - Q_C/Q_H
Always less than 1: some heat must be
dumped to the cold reservoir.

# Carnot efficiency (the ceiling)
  e_carnot = 1 - T_C/T_H
Temperatures in kelvin. No real engine
beats this, and no engine reaches it.

# Refrigerators and heat pumps
Work is put in to move heat from cold to
hot.
  COP_fridge = Q_C/W
  COP_pump = Q_H/W

# Entropy
  dS = Q/T
A measure of disorder, or of how many
microscopic arrangements match the state.

# Second law by entropy
The entropy of an isolated system never
decreases. It stays constant only for a
reversible process.

# Direction of time
Entropy is why a broken cup never
reassembles even though every underlying
collision is reversible.
