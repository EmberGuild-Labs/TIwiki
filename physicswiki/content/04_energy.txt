@CATEGORY Work and Energy

@ARTICLE Work
  W = F*d*cos(theta)
theta is the angle between the force and
the displacement. Work is a scalar
measured in joules.
  1 J = 1 N*m

# Sign
- theta = 0: positive work, energy added
- theta = 90: zero work
- theta = 180: negative work, energy
removed (friction, braking)

# Zero work situations
- Carrying a box horizontally: the lift
force is perpendicular to the motion.
- Centripetal force does no work.
- Pushing a wall that does not move.

# Variable force
  W = area under an F vs x graph
  W = integral of F dx

# Net work
  W_net = sum of the work by each force
        = F_net * d * cos(theta)

@ARTICLE Kinetic Energy
  KE = (1/2)m*v^2
Always positive, a scalar, in joules.

# Work-energy theorem
  W_net = change in KE
        = (1/2)m*v_f^2 - (1/2)m*v_i^2
Net work speeds an object up; negative
net work slows it down.

# Scaling
KE depends on v squared. Doubling the
speed quadruples the kinetic energy, and
quadruples the braking distance.

# Stopping distance
  (1/2)m*v^2 = f*d
  d = v^2/(2*mu_k*g)
Independent of mass on a flat road.

@ARTICLE Potential Energy
Stored energy that depends on position.

# Gravitational, near the surface
  U = mgh
h is measured from a reference level you
choose. Only changes in U matter, so the
choice of zero is free.

# Gravitational, general
  U = -G*M*m/r

# Elastic (spring)
  U = (1/2)k*x^2

# Conservative forces
Gravity and spring forces are
conservative: the work they do depends
only on the endpoints, and is zero around
a closed loop. Potential energy can only
be defined for these.

# Non-conservative forces
Friction and drag are not conservative.
They convert mechanical energy into
thermal energy along the path.

# Force from potential energy
  F = -dU/dx
The force points downhill on the
potential energy curve.

@ARTICLE Conservation of Energy
# With no friction
  KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f
  (1/2)m*v_i^2 + m*g*h_i
    = (1/2)m*v_f^2 + m*g*h_f

# With friction
  KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f + E_thermal
  E_thermal = f_k * d

# General statement
  E_i + W_external = E_f
Energy is never created or destroyed,
only transferred or converted.

# Dropped from rest through height h
  v = sqrt(2gh)
Same result as the kinematic equation,
and independent of mass.

# Pendulum or ramp
  mgh = (1/2)m*v^2
  v = sqrt(2gh)
The speed at the bottom depends only on
the height dropped, not the path taken,
when friction is negligible.

# When to use energy vs forces
Use energy when the question involves
speed and position and you do not care
about time. Use forces when you need
acceleration or time.

@ARTICLE Power
  P = W/t
  P = F*v
Power is the rate of doing work, in
watts.
  1 W = 1 J/s

# Useful conversions
  1 hp = 746 W
  1 kWh = 3.6e6 J

# Instantaneous power
  P = F*v*cos(theta)

# Efficiency
  efficiency = P_out/P_in * 100%
Always less than 100% in real machines;
the difference becomes heat.

# Climbing at constant speed
  P = mgh/t = mg*v_vertical

@ARTICLE Energy in Springs
# Elastic potential energy
  U = (1/2)k*x^2
x is measured from the natural length.

# Launching a mass
  (1/2)k*x^2 = (1/2)m*v^2
  v = x*sqrt(k/m)

# Onto a vertical spring
  mgh = (1/2)k*x^2
for a mass dropped from height h above
the spring, at maximum compression x
(measure h to the compressed point).

# Graph
The force vs stretch graph is a straight
line of slope k, and the area under it is
the stored energy, hence the factor 1/2.

# Energy in SHM
Energy trades between kinetic and elastic
but the total stays constant:
  E = (1/2)k*A^2
  v_max = A*sqrt(k/m)
