@CATEGORY Modern Physics

@ARTICLE Photons and the Photoelectric Effect
# Photon energy
  E = h*f = h*c/lambda
  h = 6.626e-34 J*s
  h*c = 1240 eV*nm
That last form is the fastest way to get
a photon energy in eV from a wavelength
in nanometres.

# Photoelectric effect
Light shone on a metal ejects electrons,
but only above a threshold frequency.
  KE_max = h*f - phi
phi is the work function, the minimum
energy needed to free an electron.

# Threshold
  f0 = phi/h
Below f0 NO electrons come out, however
bright the light. This is what classical
wave theory cannot explain, and it is why
Einstein won the Nobel Prize.

# Intensity vs frequency
- Brighter light: more electrons, same
maximum energy each.
- Higher frequency: more energy per
electron.

# Stopping voltage
  e*V_stop = KE_max

@ARTICLE Wave-Particle Duality
# De Broglie wavelength
  lambda = h/p = h/(m*v)
Every particle has a wavelength. It is
utterly negligible for everyday objects
and decisive for electrons.

# Evidence
Electrons diffract through crystals just
as X-rays do, which is the basis of the
electron microscope. Its resolution beats
a light microscope because the electron
wavelength is far shorter.

# Light is both
Interference and diffraction show waves;
the photoelectric effect and Compton
scattering show particles. Which face
appears depends on the experiment.

# Uncertainty principle
  dx * dp >= h/(4*pi)
  dE * dt >= h/(4*pi)
Position and momentum cannot both be
known exactly. This is a property of
nature, not a limit of our instruments.

# Compton effect
X-rays scattering off electrons lose
energy and gain wavelength, exactly as
billiard-ball photons would.

@ARTICLE Atomic Models and Spectra
# Development
- Thomson: plum pudding
- Rutherford: gold foil scattering showed
a tiny dense positive nucleus
- Bohr: quantised orbits
- Quantum mechanics: probability clouds

# Bohr model of hydrogen
  E_n = -13.6/n^2  eV
n = 1 is the ground state. The energies
are negative because the electron is
bound.

# Photon from a transition
  E_photon = E_high - E_low
  hf = E_i - E_f
Absorption lifts an electron up; emission
drops it down and releases a photon.

# Why spectra are line spectra
Only certain energies are allowed, so
only certain photon energies appear. Each
element has its own fingerprint, which is
how the composition of stars is known.

# Ionisation energy of hydrogen
  13.6 eV, from n = 1 to n = infinity

# Emission vs absorption
A hot thin gas emits bright lines. A cool
gas in front of a continuous source
absorbs the very same lines, leaving dark
gaps.

@ARTICLE Nuclear Physics
# Notation
  A = mass number (protons + neutrons)
  Z = atomic number (protons)
  N = A - Z (neutrons)
Isotopes share Z and differ in N.

# Mass defect and binding energy
The nucleus weighs less than its parts.
  E = m*c^2
  1 u = 931.5 MeV/c^2
The missing mass is the binding energy
that holds it together.

# Binding energy per nucleon
Peaks near iron-56. That is why fusing
light nuclei releases energy, and so does
splitting heavy ones.

# Fission
A heavy nucleus splits, releasing energy
and more neutrons, which can sustain a
chain reaction. This is a nuclear reactor
or a fission bomb.

# Fusion
Light nuclei merge. This powers the Sun,
which fuses hydrogen into helium, and it
releases more energy per kilogram than
fission.

# The strong force
Binds nucleons but has an extremely short
range, so very large nuclei are unstable.

@ARTICLE Radioactive Decay
# Alpha decay
Emits a helium nucleus.
  A decreases by 4, Z by 2
Stopped by paper or skin.

# Beta minus decay
A neutron becomes a proton and an
electron.
  A unchanged, Z increases by 1
Stopped by a few millimetres of aluminium.

# Beta plus decay
A proton becomes a neutron and a positron.
  A unchanged, Z decreases by 1

# Gamma decay
A photon from a nucleus dropping to a
lower state.
  A and Z unchanged
Needs thick lead or concrete.

# Half life
  N = N0 * (1/2)^(t/T_half)
  A = A0 * (1/2)^(t/T_half)
After n half lives, the fraction left is
(1/2)^n.

# Decay constant
  N = N0 * e^(-lambda*t)
  T_half = ln(2)/lambda = 0.693/lambda

# Carbon dating
Living things hold a steady ratio of
carbon-14; once dead it decays with a half
life of 5730 years, giving the age.

# Conservation rules
A and Z each balance across a nuclear
equation. Use that to identify the missing
particle.

@ARTICLE Special Relativity
# The two postulates
1. The laws of physics are the same in
every inertial frame.
2. The speed of light in vacuum is the
same for every observer, whatever their
motion.

# Lorentz factor
  gamma = 1/sqrt(1 - v^2/c^2)
gamma is 1 at rest and grows without limit
as v approaches c.

# Time dilation
  t = gamma * t_proper
Moving clocks run slow. Proper time is
measured in the frame where the two events
happen at the same place.

# Length contraction
  L = L_proper/gamma
Lengths shrink along the direction of
motion only.

# Evidence
Muons created high in the atmosphere reach
the ground even though they should decay
first. In our frame their clocks run slow;
in theirs the atmosphere is contracted.

# Relativistic energy
  E = gamma*m*c^2
  E_rest = m*c^2
  KE = (gamma - 1)*m*c^2

# Why c is a limit
Accelerating a mass to c would take
infinite energy, so only massless
particles travel at c.

# When it matters
At v = 0.1c gamma is only 1.005. Below
about 10% of c the classical formulas are
fine.
