@CATEGORY Circular Motion and Gravity

@ARTICLE Uniform Circular Motion
Constant speed on a circular path. The
velocity still changes because its
direction changes, so there is an
acceleration.

# Centripetal acceleration
  a_c = v^2/r
  a_c = 4*pi^2*r/T^2
Always points toward the center,
perpendicular to the velocity.

# Period and frequency
  T = 1/f
  v = 2*pi*r/T

# Angular speed
  omega = 2*pi/T = 2*pi*f
  v = omega*r
  a_c = omega^2 * r

# Important
Speed is constant but velocity is not.
There is no outward force; the outward
feeling is inertia carrying you straight
while the surface turns beneath you.

@ARTICLE Centripetal Force
  F_c = m*v^2/r
This is not a new force. It is the name
for the net inward force, supplied by
whatever is actually pulling or pushing.

# What supplies it
  car turning:     friction
  ball on string:  tension
  satellite:       gravity
  loop the loop:   normal + weight
  banked curve:    component of normal

# Method
1. Draw the free-body diagram.
2. Point the positive axis toward the
center.
3. Set the net inward force equal to
m*v^2/r.

# Flat curve, maximum speed
Friction supplies the whole force:
  mu_s*mg = m*v^2/r
  v_max = sqrt(mu_s*g*r)
Independent of mass.

@ARTICLE Vertical Circles and Banking
# Top of a vertical loop
Weight and normal force both point down
toward the center:
  N + mg = m*v^2/r

Minimum speed to keep contact is where
N = 0:
  v_min = sqrt(g*r)

# Bottom of the loop
  N - mg = m*v^2/r
  N = mg + m*v^2/r
This is why you feel heaviest at the
bottom.

# Banked curve, no friction
  tan(theta) = v^2/(r*g)
The ideal speed for a given bank angle.
Also independent of mass.

# Ball on a string, horizontal circle
The string cannot be truly horizontal;
the vertical component of tension must
support the weight.
  T*cos(theta) = mg
  T*sin(theta) = m*v^2/r

@ARTICLE Newton's Law of Gravitation
  F = G*m1*m2/r^2
  G = 6.674e-11 N*m^2/kg^2

# Key points
- r is measured center to center, not
surface to surface.
- The force is attractive and acts along
the line joining the masses.
- Inverse square: triple the distance and
the force drops to 1/9.
- The two forces are an action-reaction
pair, equal in size on both bodies.

# Gravitational field
  g = G*M/r^2
The acceleration due to gravity at
distance r from the center of mass M.
At Earth's surface r is Earth's radius.

# Weight from gravitation
  mg = G*M*m/R^2

# Gravitational potential energy
  U = -G*M*m/r
Zero at infinity and negative elsewhere.
Near the surface this reduces to the
familiar U = mgh.

@ARTICLE Orbits and Kepler's Laws
# Orbital speed (circular orbit)
Gravity supplies the centripetal force:
  G*M*m/r^2 = m*v^2/r
  v = sqrt(G*M/r)
Independent of the satellite's mass.

# Orbital period
  T^2 = 4*pi^2*r^3/(G*M)

# Escape speed
  v_esc = sqrt(2*G*M/r)
About 11.2 km/s from Earth's surface.
Note v_esc = sqrt(2) * v_orbit.

# Kepler's first law
Planets move in ellipses with the Sun at
one focus.

# Kepler's second law
A line from the Sun to the planet sweeps
equal areas in equal times, so planets
move fastest at perihelion (closest) and
slowest at aphelion.

# Kepler's third law
  T^2 is proportional to r^3
For two bodies orbiting the same star:
  (T1/T2)^2 = (r1/r2)^3

# Weightlessness in orbit
Astronauts are not beyond gravity; they
are in continuous free fall together with
the station, so the normal force is zero.
