@CATEGORY Optics and Light

@ARTICLE Electromagnetic Waves
# Speed of light
  c = 3.00e8 m/s in vacuum
  c = f * lambda
Light is a transverse wave of oscillating
electric and magnetic fields at right
angles to each other and to the direction
of travel. It needs no medium.

# The spectrum, long to short
  radio, microwave, infrared, visible,
  ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma
Longer wavelength means lower frequency
and lower photon energy.

# Visible light
  about 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red)
Red has the longest wavelength and the
lowest energy of the visible colours.

# Photon energy
  E = h*f = h*c/lambda
  h = 6.626e-34 J*s

# Intensity
  I = P/A
Falls off as an inverse square from a
point source.

@ARTICLE Reflection and Mirrors
# Law of reflection
  angle of incidence = angle of
  reflection
Both measured from the normal.

# Plane mirror
The image is virtual, upright, the same
size, and as far behind the mirror as the
object is in front. It is laterally
inverted.

# Concave mirror (converging)
  f = R/2, positive
- Object beyond C: real, inverted,
smaller
- Between C and F: real, inverted, larger
- Inside F: virtual, upright, larger (a
shaving or make-up mirror)

# Convex mirror (diverging)
  f is negative
Always virtual, upright and smaller, with
a wide field of view. Used for security
mirrors and passenger side mirrors, hence
"objects are closer than they appear".

# Ray rules for mirrors
- A ray parallel to the axis reflects
through F.
- A ray through F reflects parallel.
- A ray to the vertex reflects
symmetrically.

@ARTICLE Refraction and Snell's Law
# Index of refraction
  n = c/v
  n >= 1 always
  vacuum 1.00, air 1.00, water 1.33,
  glass ~1.5, diamond 2.42

# Snell's law
  n1*sin(theta1) = n2*sin(theta2)

# Bending direction
Entering a denser medium (larger n) the
light slows and bends TOWARD the normal.
Leaving into a less dense medium it bends
away.

# What changes
The speed and the wavelength change; the
frequency never does.
  lambda_n = lambda_vacuum/n

# Everyday effects
A straw looks bent in a glass, pools look
shallower than they are, and stars twinkle
as air of varying density refracts their
light.

# Dispersion
n depends slightly on wavelength, so violet
bends more than red. A prism separates
white light, and raindrops make rainbows.

@ARTICLE Total Internal Reflection
Happens only when going from a denser to a
less dense medium (n1 > n2).

# Critical angle
  sin(theta_c) = n2/n1
At exactly theta_c the refracted ray grazes
along the surface. Beyond it, no light
escapes at all: it is 100% reflected.

# Water to air
  theta_c = 48.8 degrees

# Applications
- Optical fibres carry light for
kilometres by repeated total internal
reflection.
- Diamonds sparkle because their high n
gives a tiny critical angle of 24 degrees,
trapping light inside.
- Prisms in binoculars replace mirrors.
- Mirages: hot air near the road is less
dense, and light from the sky curves up
into your eye.

@ARTICLE Lenses
# Converging (convex) lens
  f is positive
Thicker in the middle. Brings parallel
rays to a focus.
- Object beyond 2f: real, inverted,
smaller (a camera)
- At 2f: real, inverted, same size
- Between f and 2f: real, inverted, larger
(a projector)
- Inside f: virtual, upright, larger (a
magnifying glass)

# Diverging (concave) lens
  f is negative
Thinner in the middle. The image is always
virtual, upright and smaller.

# Ray rules for lenses
- A ray parallel to the axis refracts
through F on the far side.
- A ray through the center goes straight
on.
- A ray through the near F emerges
parallel.

# The eye
Nearsighted eyes focus in front of the
retina and are corrected with a diverging
lens; farsighted eyes need a converging
one.

# Power of a lens
  P = 1/f   (dioptres, f in metres)

@ARTICLE Mirror and Lens Equations
# The equation (mirrors and lenses)
  1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i

# Magnification
  m = -d_i/d_o = h_i/h_o
  |m| > 1 enlarged
  |m| < 1 reduced
  m negative: inverted and real
  m positive: upright and virtual

# Sign conventions
  f > 0: converging lens, concave
         mirror
  f < 0: diverging lens, convex mirror
  d_i > 0: real image (far side for a
           lens, front for a mirror)
  d_i < 0: virtual image

# Reading the answer
A negative d_i always means a virtual,
upright image on the same side as the
object. Let the signs do the work rather
than deciding the answer in advance.

@ARTICLE Interference and Diffraction
# Double slit
  d*sin(theta) = n*lambda    (bright)
  d*sin(theta) = (n+1/2)lambda  (dark)
  y = n*lambda*L/d
d is the slit separation and L the
distance to the screen.

# What it proves
Only waves interfere, so Young's
experiment established the wave nature of
light.

# Diffraction grating
  d*sin(theta) = n*lambda
Same relation but with thousands of slits,
giving very sharp, widely spaced maxima.
  d = 1/(lines per metre)

# Single slit (dark fringes)
  a*sin(theta) = n*lambda,  n = 1,2,3
The central bright band is twice as wide
as the others. Narrower slits spread the
pattern MORE.

# Thin films
An extra 180 degree phase shift occurs on
reflection from a denser medium. This is
why soap bubbles and oil slicks show
colour, and how anti-reflective coatings
work.

# Polarisation
  I = I0*cos^2(theta)   (Malus's law)
Unpolarised light through a first filter
drops to half its intensity. Only
transverse waves can be polarised, so this
proves light is transverse.
