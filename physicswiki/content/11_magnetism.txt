@CATEGORY Magnetism

@ARTICLE Magnetic Force on a Charge
  F = q*v*B*sin(theta)
theta is the angle between v and B. Units
of B are teslas.
  1 T = 10^4 gauss

# Direction: right hand rule
Point the fingers along v, curl them
toward B, and the thumb gives F for a
POSITIVE charge. Reverse it for a
negative charge.

# Key features
- No force on a stationary charge.
- No force if v is parallel to B.
- The force is always perpendicular to v,
so it changes direction but never speed.
- Therefore a magnetic force does NO work.

# Circular motion in a field
  q*v*B = m*v^2/r
  r = m*v/(q*B)
  T = 2*pi*m/(q*B)
The period does not depend on the speed,
which is the principle of the cyclotron.

# Velocity selector
Crossed E and B fields let through only
one speed:
  qE = qvB,  so  v = E/B

@ARTICLE Force on a Wire
  F = B*I*L*sin(theta)
A current-carrying wire in a magnetic
field feels a force, because it is a
stream of moving charges.

# Direction
Right hand rule again, with the fingers
pointing along the conventional current.

# Motors
The forces on the two sides of a current
loop are opposite, so the loop
experiences a torque and spins. A
commutator flips the current twice per
revolution to keep it turning.

# Torque on a loop
  tau = N*B*I*A*sin(theta)
N is the number of turns and A the loop
area.

# Two parallel wires
  F/L = mu0*I1*I2/(2*pi*d)
Currents in the same direction attract;
opposite directions repel. This is the
definition of the ampere.

@ARTICLE Sources of Magnetic Field
  mu0 = 4*pi*1e-7 T*m/A

# Long straight wire
  B = mu0*I/(2*pi*r)
Circular field lines around the wire.
Grip the wire with the right hand, thumb
along the current, and the fingers curl
the way B points.

# Center of a loop
  B = mu0*I/(2*R)
For N turns, multiply by N.

# Solenoid
  B = mu0*n*I
n is turns per metre. The field inside is
nearly uniform and outside it is close to
zero.

# Ampere's law
  sum of B*dL = mu0 * I_enclosed
The magnetic analogue of Gauss's law,
used for symmetric current arrangements.

# No magnetic monopoles
Field lines always form closed loops.
Cutting a magnet in half gives two
magnets, never an isolated north pole.

@ARTICLE Induction and Faraday's Law
# Magnetic flux
  flux = B*A*cos(theta)
Units webers.

# Faraday's law
  emf = -N * (change in flux)/(time)
A CHANGING flux induces a voltage. A
steady field, however strong, induces
nothing.

# Three ways to change the flux
- Change B (move a magnet)
- Change A (squeeze the loop)
- Change theta (rotate the loop, which is
how a generator works)

# Lenz's law
The minus sign. The induced current
opposes the change that produced it.

# Using Lenz's law
Push a magnet toward a coil and the coil
pushes back; pull it away and the coil
pulls it back. This is conservation of
energy, and it is why generators are hard
to turn under load.

# Motional emf
  emf = B*L*v
for a rod of length L sliding at speed v
across a field.

@ARTICLE Inductance and AC
# Self inductance
  emf = -L * (change in I)/(time)
Units henries. An inductor resists
CHANGES in current, much as mass resists
changes in velocity.

# Energy stored
  U = (1/2)L*I^2

# Transformers
  V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p
  I_s/I_p = N_p/N_s
  P_in = P_out   (ideal)
Step up the voltage and the current steps
down. Transformers only work on AC,
because they need a changing flux.

# Why the grid uses high voltage
Power loss in the lines is I^2*R, so
raising V and lowering I for the same
delivered power cuts the losses sharply.

# AC quantities
  V_rms = V_peak/sqrt(2)
  I_rms = I_peak/sqrt(2)
Mains voltage quoted as 120 V or 230 V is
the rms value.

# Reactance
  X_L = 2*pi*f*L
  X_C = 1/(2*pi*f*C)
Inductors block high frequencies,
capacitors block low ones.

# Resonance in an LC circuit
  f = 1/(2*pi*sqrt(L*C))
This is how a radio is tuned.
